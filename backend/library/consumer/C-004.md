## Excerpt (verbatim quotes from the source PDF; every quoted fragment is machine-checked)
s.34(1) District Commission: "the District Commission shall have jurisdiction to entertain complaints where the value of the goods or services paid as consideration does not exceed one crore rupees"
s.34(1) proviso: "Provided that where the Central Government deems it necessary so to do, it may prescribe such other value, as it deems fit"
s.47(1)(a)(i) State Commission: "complaints where the value of the goods or services paid as consideration, exceeds rupees one crore, but does not exceed rupees ten crore"
s.58(1)(a)(i) National Commission: "complaints where the value of the goods or services paid as consideration exceeds rupees ten crore"

## Plain-language meaning (my words)
The Act's own default limits are: District up to Rs 1 crore, State Rs 1 to 10 crore, National above Rs 10 crore, measured on what you PAID. But each section lets the Central Government prescribe other values, and it did in 2021 (see the secondary-source section). Current working limits: District up to Rs 50 lakh, State above 50 lakh up to Rs 2 crore, National above Rs 2 crore.

## Limits (what this does NOT say)
The Act's numbers alone are OUTDATED; answers must use the Rules' numbers. The Rules text has not been checked against the Gazette. The limit depends on the price paid, not the compensation claimed.

Consumer Protection (Jurisdiction of the District Commission, the State Commission and the National Commission) Rules, 2021, notified 30 December 2021 (per the article):
District Commission: "does not exceed fifty lakh rupees"
State Commission: "exceeds fifty lakh but does not exceed two crore rupees"
National Commission: "exceeds two crore rupees"
A June 2025 article says the Supreme Court upheld the "consideration paid" basis (Rutur Mihir Panchal v. Union of India, W.P.(C) 282 of 2021). Do not cite the case until the judgment itself is read.
