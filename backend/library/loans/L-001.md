## Excerpt (verbatim quotes from the source PDF; every quoted fragment is machine-checked)
s.13(1): "any security interest created in favour of any secured creditor may be enforced, without the intervention of court or tribunal, by such creditor in accordance with the provisions of this Act"
s.13(2): "his account in respect of such debt is classified by the secured creditor as non-performing asset"
s.13(2): "may require the borrower by notice in writing to discharge in full his liabilities to the secured creditor within sixty days from the date of notice failing which the secured creditor shall be entitled to exercise all or any of the rights under sub-section (4)"

## Plain-language meaning (my words)
For a SECURED loan (one backed by property or other security), once the lender marks the account a non-performing asset (NPA), it can send a written notice giving the borrower 60 days from the date of the notice to pay in full. If the borrower does not, the lender can use the measures in s.13(4) without going to court.

## Limits (what this does NOT say)
Applies only to secured debts and secured creditors (banks and financial institutions covered by the Act; those definitions are not quoted). Unsecured loans such as credit cards or personal loans are NOT covered, so a notice about those is a different case. The 60 days run 'from the date of notice', not from receipt. Exceptions are in L-006. Recovery-agent conduct is RBI guidance and is not in this source.
