## Excerpt (verbatim quotes from the source PDF; every quoted fragment is machine-checked)
s.138: "is returned by the bank unpaid, either because of the amount of money standing to the credit of that account is insufficient to honour the cheque or that it exceeds the amount arranged to be paid from that account by an agreement made with that bank"
s.138: "such person shall be deemed to have committed an offence"
s.138 punishment: "a term which may be extended to two years"
s.138 punishment: "or with fine which may extend to twice the amount of the cheque, or with both"
s.138 Explanation: "means a legally enforceable debt or other liability"

## Plain-language meaning (my words)
If a cheque given to pay a legally enforceable debt bounces for insufficient funds (or because it exceeds the arranged limit), the person who issued it can be punished with up to two years in jail, a fine up to twice the cheque amount, or both. This is the offence only; section 138 applies only if the conditions in N-002 are met.

## Limits (what this does NOT say)
Covers bounce for insufficient funds or exceeding the arranged limit. Other bounce reasons are not covered by this text. Only a debt or liability that is legally enforceable counts. Punishment is the maximum the court MAY give, never automatic. The bot must not predict the outcome.
