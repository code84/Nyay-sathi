## Excerpt (verbatim quotes from the source PDF; every quoted fragment is machine-checked)
s.138 proviso: "Provided that nothing contained in this section shall apply unless"
proviso (a): "the cheque has been presented to the bank within a period of six months from the date on which it is drawn or within the period of its validity, whichever is earlier"
proviso (b): "makes a demand for the payment of the said amount of money by giving a notice; in writing, to the drawer of the cheque"
proviso (b): "within thirty days"
proviso (b): "of the receipt of information by him from the bank regarding the return of the cheque as unpaid"
proviso (c): "the drawer of such cheque fails to make the payment of the said amount of money to the payee or, as the case may be, to the holder in due course of the cheque, within fifteen days of the receipt of the said notice"

## Plain-language meaning (my words)
For a cheque-bounce case to proceed, (a) the cheque must have been presented to the bank in time, (b) the payee must send a WRITTEN demand notice within 30 days of getting the bank's information that the cheque bounced, and (c) the drawer must then fail to pay within 15 days of receiving that notice. If the payee misses step (a) or (b), section 138 does not apply. If the drawer pays within the 15 days, the offence is not made out under (c).

## Limits (what this does NOT say)
The 30 days run from when the payee RECEIVES the bank's information; the 15 days run from when the drawer RECEIVES the notice, and the date of receipt can be disputed (how notices are served is not covered here). Cheque validity under banking rules is shorter than six months in practice, but that rule is NOT in this source and must not be stated until separately sourced.
