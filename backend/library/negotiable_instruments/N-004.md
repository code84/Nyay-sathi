## Excerpt (verbatim quotes from the source PDF; every quoted fragment is machine-checked)
s.142(1)(a): "no court shall take cognizance of any offence punishable under section 138 except upon a complaint, in writing, made by the payee or, as the case may be, the holder in due course of the cheque"
s.142(1)(b): "such complaint is made within one month of the date on which the cause of action arises under clause (c) of the proviso to section 138"
s.142(1)(b) proviso: "the cognizance of a complaint may be taken by the Court after the prescribed period, if the complainant satisfies the Court that he had sufficient cause for not making a complaint within such period"
s.142(1)(c): "no court inferior to that of a Metropolitan Magistrate or a Judicial Magistrate of the first class shall try any offence punishable under section 138"

## Plain-language meaning (my words)
Only the payee (or holder in due course) can file, in writing. The complaint must be made within ONE MONTH after the 15-day payment window ends (the cause of action arises under clause (c) of the proviso). A late complaint can still be taken up if the complainant shows sufficient cause. The trial court is a Metropolitan Magistrate or Judicial Magistrate of the first class.

## Limits (what this does NOT say)
'Cause of action arises under clause (c)' means the day after the 15 days lapse without payment; computing the exact date is a job for code plus a lawyer's check, since receipt dates can be disputed. Condonation of delay is the court's choice, never guaranteed.
